# PickMe Gwanda Growth And Expansion Plan

Report date: 2026-06-12

Scope: Business readiness, growth readiness, driver acquisition, rider acquisition, retention, revenue, and city expansion

Current status:

- Technical platform: approved for controlled Gwanda pilot
- Current readiness score: 91/100
- Initial pilot: 5 drivers and 10 riders
- Launch city: Gwanda, Zimbabwe

## Executive Summary

PickMe should launch in Gwanda as a trust-first, locally operated ride-hailing network before trying to behave like a national app. The first objective is not raw downloads. The first objective is dependable liquidity: enough verified drivers and repeat riders in the same few high-frequency corridors that users can reliably get a ride.

The recommended strategy is:

- Start with tightly managed driver and rider cohorts.
- Build supply before demand.
- Win trust through safety, reliability, transparent pricing, local support, and predictable wallet controls.
- Grow Gwanda corridor by corridor.
- Expand to Bulawayo only after Gwanda proves repeat usage, driver earnings, operational discipline, and financial reconciliation.

## 1. Driver Acquisition Strategy

### Driver Recruitment Strategy

Priority driver segments:

- Existing taxi drivers around town ranks, shopping areas, clinics, schools, and bus connection points.
- Part-time private-car drivers with clean vehicles and local references.
- Drivers already serving hotels, lodges, churches, schools, and small businesses.
- Retired or semi-retired professional drivers.
- Drivers operating on the Bulawayo-Gwanda and Gwanda-Beitbridge corridor who can later support intercity products.

Recruitment channels:

- In-person driver sessions at known pickup points.
- Referrals from the first approved drivers.
- Partnerships with car washes, fuel stations, garages, and tyre shops.
- WhatsApp driver groups.
- Local business associations.
- Church/community leader introductions.
- Small posters at service stations and transport-adjacent businesses.

Driver pitch:

- More predictable ride demand.
- Lower idle time.
- Local support team that knows Gwanda.
- Transparent commission.
- Wallet-backed payment option.
- Early-driver status with launch incentives.
- Opportunity to shape the platform before larger competitors arrive.

### Driver Onboarding Process

Recommended onboarding flow:

1. Driver lead captured by operations.
2. Phone screening.
3. Document collection.
4. Vehicle inspection.
5. Identity verification.
6. Driver authorization approval.
7. App training.
8. Wallet and settlement training.
9. Safety and conduct briefing.
10. First supervised ride.
11. First-week performance review.

Driver onboarding session should cover:

- How to accept rides.
- How to handle rider pickup and no-show.
- How wallet rides work.
- What to do when app, websocket, or location updates fail.
- Driver conduct expectations.
- Fraud and collusion rules.
- Support escalation path.
- How ratings and suspension work.

### Verification Process

Minimum verification:

- National ID.
- Valid driver license.
- Vehicle registration or proof of permission to operate vehicle.
- Vehicle photos.
- Proof of insurance where applicable.
- Phone number verification.
- Local reference check.
- In-person vehicle check.
- Driver face/photo match.

Operational checks:

- Vehicle cleanliness.
- Seat belts.
- Tyres.
- Lights.
- Phone mount.
- Ability to use app and GPS.
- Knowledge of Gwanda routes.

Disqualification triggers:

- Mismatched identity.
- Unsafe vehicle.
- Refusal to follow platform rules.
- Unresolved complaint from local references.
- Attempt to use another driver identity.

### Driver Incentives

Week 1 incentives:

- 0% commission on first 5 completed pilot rides per driver.
- Fuel voucher for drivers completing 10 quality rides with no serious complaint.
- Priority listing for drivers with high acceptance and completion.
- Driver referral reward after referred driver completes 10 approved rides.

Month 1 incentives:

- Reduced commission tier for founding drivers with strong reliability.
- Weekly recognition for top-rated driver.
- Small airtime/fuel bonus for peak-hour availability.
- Fast support channel for founding drivers.

Avoid:

- Large unsustainable cash guarantees.
- Incentives that reward fake rides.
- Bonuses based only on ride count without fraud checks.

### First 30 Driver Targets

Target composition:

- 10 full-time taxi/professional drivers.
- 10 part-time local private-car drivers.
- 5 business/hotel-connected drivers.
- 5 reserve drivers for peaks and backup.

Quality targets:

- 90% documents complete.
- 100% identity verified.
- 100% vehicle inspected.
- 80% weekly active drivers.
- Less than 5% serious complaint rate.
- Median pickup time under 10 minutes in core Gwanda areas.

### First 100 Driver Targets

Target composition:

- 50 Gwanda town drivers.
- 20 corridor/intercity-capable drivers.
- 15 business/hotel drivers.
- 15 reserve/peak drivers.

Expansion rule:

Do not recruit 100 drivers unless rider demand supports earnings. Oversupplying drivers damages trust faster than slow expansion.

Driver health targets before 100 drivers:

- At least 4 completed rides per active driver per day in core hours.
- Less than 10 minute median pickup in core zones.
- Less than 15% driver churn month-over-month.
- No unresolved driver payout or wallet dispute.

### Referral Strategy

Rider referral mechanics:

- Founding riders get a referral code.
- Referrer earns ride credit after referred rider completes first valid ride.
- Referred rider earns first-ride discount or wallet credit.
- Rewards must be capped and fraud-reviewed.

Controls:

- One reward per verified phone/user.
- No reward for self-referral.
- No reward until ride completes.
- Fraud review for repeated device/payment patterns.

## 3. Gwanda Growth Plan

### Growth Model

PickMe should grow through density, not geography first.

Stage 1: Controlled pilot

- 5 drivers.
- 10 riders.
- Manual monitoring.
- Daily reconciliation.
- Direct support.

Stage 2: Early liquidity

- 10 drivers.
- 20 riders.
- Cover core town zones.
- Prove pickup reliability.
- Prove repeat usage.

Stage 3: Managed expansion

- 20 drivers.
- 50 riders.
- Requires pilot limit expansion approval.
- Add campus/business segments.
- Add more operating hours.

Stage 4: Local network

- 50 drivers.
- 100+ riders.
- Requires proven demand and operational readiness.
- Add events, business accounts, and corridor rides.

### Service Quality Metrics

Do not expand unless these are healthy:

- Median pickup ETA under 10 minutes in core zones.
- Driver acceptance rate above 75%.
- Ride completion rate above 85%.
- Cancellation rate below 15%.
- Support complaints resolved same day.
- Zero unresolved financial variance.
- Zero duplicate credits.
- Driver earnings remain attractive.

### Supply-Demand Balance

Recommended ratio:

- Early pilot: 1 active driver per 2 to 4 active riders.
- Growing market: 1 active driver per 5 to 8 weekly active riders.
- Mature local market: 1 active driver per 8 to 12 weekly active riders.

If pickup times are high:

- Recruit more drivers in that zone or time window.
- Add driver peak incentives.
- Reduce broad marketing until supply improves.

If driver earnings are too low:

- Slow driver recruitment.
- Focus on rider activation.
- Add business and student demand.

## 4. Revenue Strategy

### Platform Fee

Current PickMe platform fee: 15%.

Recommendation:

- Keep 15% as the standard fee.
- Offer temporary founding-driver commission relief for launch.
- Avoid a permanent race to the bottom.
- Reinvest early revenue into driver support, safety, operations, and rider acquisition.

Why 15% can work:

- It is simple.
- It supports operations.
- It leaves room for driver incentives.
- It is competitive if PickMe provides real demand, safety, and support.

### Wallet Revenue

Near-term:

- Wallet should be treated as a trust and retention tool, not a primary revenue source.
- Avoid hidden wallet fees during pilot.
- Do not monetize wallet balances aggressively.

Future:

- Small business account convenience fees.
- Instant settlement fees only if optional and transparent.
- Provider-funded promotions.
- Float or treasury revenue only with full legal and regulatory review.

### Business Rides

Most promising medium-term revenue line:

- Staff transport.
- Hotel guest rides.
- Mine/contractor rides.
- School transport coordination.
- Monthly business billing.
- Priority support subscription for businesses.

Recommended model:

- Standard ride fee plus platform commission.
- Later add business account fee for monthly reporting and support.

### Future Advertising

Potential channels:

- In-app local business promotions.
- Driver vehicle advertising.
- Sponsored destination cards.
- Local event promotions.

Rule:

Advertising must not degrade trust, safety, or app usability.

### Delivery Opportunities

Future verticals:

- Parcel delivery.
- Pharmacy delivery.
- Food delivery from local restaurants.
- Business document delivery.
- Intercity parcel handoff on Gwanda-Bulawayo route.

Recommendation:

Do not launch delivery until ride reliability is proven. Delivery can become a strong driver utilization product after the ride network has density.

## 6. Competitive Positioning

### PickMe vs inDrive

inDrive is known globally for peer-to-peer fare negotiation and a large international footprint. Its model can appeal to price-sensitive riders and drivers who want more control over fare negotiation.

PickMe advantages:

- Local Gwanda-first focus.
- Stronger local support.
- Controlled wallet and pilot safety.
- Ability to build trust through verified local drivers.
- Can adapt to Zimbabwean town-by-town realities faster.

PickMe weaknesses:

- Lower brand awareness.
- Smaller driver network.
- Lower marketing budget.
- Must prove reliability from zero.

### PickMe vs Bolt

Bolt is a major global ride-hailing platform with scale, app maturity, and broad operational experience.

PickMe advantages:

- Can enter smaller towns before global players prioritize them.
- Can build community trust locally.
- Can focus on business rides and local relationships.
- Can operate with leaner local tactics.

PickMe weaknesses:

- Less capital.
- Less brand recognition.
- Less mature marketplace tooling.

### PickMe vs Traditional Taxis

Traditional taxis have existing trust, local knowledge, and physical presence.

PickMe advantages:

- Digital request flow.
- Driver verification.
- Trip records.
- Wallet controls.
- Potentially better dispatch and support.
- Transparent pilot rules.

PickMe weaknesses:

- Riders may already have trusted phone contacts.
- Drivers may resist commission.
- App adoption may be slow.

### PickMe vs Local Transport Operators

Local operators understand demand patterns and relationships deeply.

PickMe advantages:

- Better data.
- Potentially better safety controls.
- Ability to organize fragmented supply.
- Future business billing and wallet rails.

PickMe weaknesses:

- Must avoid being seen as threatening local livelihoods.
- Needs partnerships, not only disruption.

## Management Recommendation

Launch Gwanda with a small, high-touch cohort. Do not chase vanity downloads. The first win is dependable local liquidity: riders can get rides, drivers earn enough to stay, finance closes cleanly, and support can solve issues quickly.

PickMe becomes a leading Zimbabwean ride-hailing platform by winning trust town by town, not by copying global competitors too early.
